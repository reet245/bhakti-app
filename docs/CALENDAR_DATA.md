# Calendar data and artwork

Reviewed 23 October 2026.

## Free calculation path

The app uses the pinned **panchang-ts 5.4.0** library for Panchang and Hindu festival calculations on the device. It requires no account, API key, subscription, server request or ephemeris download. The dependency has no runtime dependencies and is MIT licensed. Its copyright and permission notice must remain with redistributed copies. The npm dependency includes that license.

- [Source and license](https://github.com/ishankgupta95/panchang)
- [Integration and supported platforms](https://dharmagya.app/docs/panchang/getting-started)
- [Published calculation accuracy and conventions](https://dharmagya.app/docs/panchang/accuracy)
- [Performance and React Native guidance](https://dharmagya.app/docs/panchang/performance)

The calculation inputs are Gregorian date, latitude, longitude and IANA timezone. The app uses Lahiri ayanamsa and Purnimanta month naming, also exposing Amanta names in the daily view. Hindu day data is based on local sunrise. Time values must be formatted in the selected location's timezone; the device timezone is not an alternative. In particular, the library's yearly festival `date` is an instant at local midnight, so converting it directly with `toISOString().slice(0, 10)` shifts Indian dates back a day.

The library computes tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana, Sun/Moon events, signs, lunar months, Samvat, daily time windows and many festival rules. These are calculated traditional calendar values, not predictions generated by an LLM. The [IMD Rashtriya Panchang explanation](https://packolkata.imd.gov.in/panchang/en/explanation) documents the astronomical basis, ending-time convention and Amanta month reckoning. [Drik Panchang's reading guide](https://www.drikpanchang.com/faq/faq-ans1.html?lang=en) explains local end times and timezone handling. These sources are validation references, not a scraped production data feed.

## Why not depend on a free hosted API?

The existing Java backend has a VedAstro PanchangaTable integration. [VedAstro's current official API documentation](https://vedastro.github.io/) lists only **5 free calls per minute**. Its table alone does not supply the complete frontend contract or a month of daily records. [Prokerala](https://client-api.prokerala.com/pricing) lists **5,000 free credits/month and 5 requests/minute**; credits are not necessarily requests. Neither free quota is a sustainable basis for every user's monthly calendar.

[Astronomy Engine](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy) is also free and MIT licensed, with Sun/Moon coordinates and rise/set calculations. It would require a separate, extensively tested Hindu calendar and festival rule implementation. panchang-ts already supplies that layer. Swiss Ephemeris offers a [choice of AGPL or a professional license](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephinfo_e.htm); it was not added to this app.

This removes per-request Panchang charges. App-store accounts, distribution and other existing app services can still have their own costs.

## Festival identity, scope and corrections

`src/features/panchang/festivals.js` enriches calculated records with the existing stories and art. `key` identifies an observance, `storyId` identifies its narrative, and `id` is `YYYY-MM-DD:key`. A narrative's old 2026 date never replaces a calculated date. The annual cache is limited to eight sets of year/location/language inputs. Annual browsing supports 1900–2100; the broad range is computational support, not a claim that every festival has been externally verified for every year.

Computed Hindu festivals in the daily Panchang use the selected location and the North Indian (`uttar-pradesh`) festival profile. Changing the city changes astronomical inputs; it does not silently change the household's religious tradition. Fixed Gregorian observances have `dateBasis: fixed`. Good Friday and Easter now use Gregorian computus for each supported year (`dateBasis: gregorian-computus`), with 2026–2028 fixtures checked against USCCB/USNO calendars. Existing Mahavir Jayanti, Buddha Purnima and Guru Nanak Jayanti entries remain explicitly labelled **2026 India reference dates**. The library is not a comprehensive regional or interfaith calendar.

### Upcoming Festivals selection

`featuredFestivals.js` provides a separate annual selection for Upcoming Festivals. It queries all engine regions at the selected coordinates, then includes explicit annual festival keys instead of filtering on the engine's `major` type (which also includes monthly Sankashti). The daily Panchang retains its full observance list. Upcoming, Past and All festivals operate within the selected year.

The selected Ekadashis are Nirjala, Devshayani, Devutthana, Mokshada and Vaikuntha. Same-name, same-date Smarta/Vaishnava observances share one card with both traditions retained in its details. Different fasting dates remain separate. Vaikuntha is supplied by a dated regional source; it is not an alias of every Mokshada/Putrada occurrence. The published calendar has a Vaikuntha occurrence on 20 December 2026 and none in Gregorian 2027.

`festivalReferencesNorth.js` and `festivalReferencesRegional.js` fill 26 coverage gaps with reviewed 2026–2027 records, including their source URLs and reference locations. `regionalFestivalReferences.js` expands only explicitly supplied dates. These entries use `dateBasis: regional-reference`; their detail pages name the source region instead of claiming to calculate the user's local observance. They are not repeated into later years. Nuakhai 2027 uses a regional almanac transcription at OdishaCalendar; most other records use individual Drik Panchang pages, with additional published festival/government sources where specified in each record.

Bonalu's announced 2026 Golconda opening is 16 July; the engine's generic Ashadha Sunday rule is not used. The 2027 schedule remains marked **Dates to be confirmed**, rather than assigned an invented date. Raja Sankranti is supplied as the verified 15 June reference in both years. Distinct annual celebrations retain their identity: Kojagari versus Diwali Lakshmi Puja, Holika Dahan versus Holi, and Kartik Purnima versus Dev Deepawali. The latter two even have different published 2027 dates. Chhath and Bathukamma stages also remain distinct.

The bundled narrative dates for Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj were corrected to **10 and 11 November 2026** to match Delhi ritual observance. The [Delhi festival calendar](https://www.drikpanchang.com/calendars/hindu/hinducalendar.html?geoname-id=1273294&year=2026) and [government holiday calendar](https://www.mpa.gov.in/sites/default/files/mpacalendar2026_5.0.pdf) differ on Govardhan: the government holiday is **9 November**. A public holiday and a ritual observance are different data concepts; the app does not present the calculated festival list as an official government holiday schedule.

## Known limits and verification

- **Sharad Purnima 2026:** the library returns 26 October for Delhi; the published moonrise observance is 25 October. The adapter applies a documented, dated reference override only within 0.1 degrees of New Delhi (28.6139, 77.2090) with timezone `Asia/Kolkata`. That record has `dateBasis: verified-reference`, a source URL and the original computed date. It appears on 25 October in daily, monthly and annual views. No correction is extrapolated to other cities or years.
- **Sampled Delhi 2027 corrections:** the [published Delhi 2027 calendar](https://www.drikpanchang.com/calendars/hindu/hinducalendar.html?geoname-id=1273294&year=2027) places Holika Dahan on 21 March, Holi on 22 March, Sarva Pitru/Mahalaya Amavasya on 29 September and Sharad Purnima on 14 October. The library returns the following day for each. These four records use the same narrowly bounded Delhi reference-override mechanism, retaining the original calculated date and source. This is a finite verified set, not a general one-day shift or a comprehensive festival audit.
- **Raja festival:** with `region: all`, version 5.4.0 incorrectly emits the 2026 Raja sequence in July. The [Odisha government calendar](https://revenue.odisha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-11/42428.pdf) lists Raja Sankranti on 15 June. The daily North Indian profile excludes these rules; the Upcoming Festivals selector excludes the faulty engine records in every year and uses verified June references for 2026–2027 only.
- **Janmashtami:** the installed library's yearly and daily APIs were checked for Delhi: 4 September 2026 and 25 August 2027 are present. Use the daily/yearly festival API with the selected timezone; a single-instant query or UTC-converted date is not an equivalent calendar query.
- **Narratives:** current long stories are English. Hindi festival names and dates come from the engine; the detail screen identifies the language of an English story. Events without a narrative show their available description, without an invented story.
- **Moonrise and polar regions:** a Moon rise/set can legitimately be absent on a civil date. A day without sunrise cannot supply a sunrise-based Hindu day. Absence must not be replaced with a guessed time.
- **Traditions:** Smarta and Vaishnava fast dates can differ. They retain distinct type labels. Daily muhurtas are traditional time windows, not a personalized marriage or ceremony recommendation.
- **Ekadashi names:** version 5.4.0's festival implementation feeds an Amanta month index to its Purnimanta Ekadashi-name table, so ordinary Krishna-paksha names are one month behind (for example, 14 January 2026 is incorrectly described as Saphala). The adapter derives the name from the public sunrise lunar coordinates: advance the Amanta month by one for Krishna paksha, keep the same month for Shukla, and use Padmini/Parama during an adhika lunation. Dates and Smarta/Vaishnava selection remain the library's calculated values; exceptional fast instructions are retained. Tests compare sampled labels with the [Delhi 2026 calendar](https://www.drikpanchang.com/calendars/hindu/hinducalendar.html?geoname-id=1273294&year=2026), including Shattila, both adhika names and a deferred Vaishnava date. `nameBasis: lunar-month-and-paksha` records this normalization.
- **Engine upgrades:** pin versions and re-run fixtures before upgrading. Upstream's accuracy claims are its published test results, not independent certification. App tests cover 2026/2027 festival examples, local date handling, Hindi identity, source distinctions and narrative/date separation; they are not exhaustive religious validation.

## Artwork and symbols

Existing app festival/deity assets are reused through `festivalImages` and the app's deity map. Their original ownership/license was not established in repository metadata during this task; retaining them does not certify their rights. The reference screenshots' Nithra branding, calendar layout, advertisements and images were not copied.

No runtime image API is needed. If new portraits are required, create original still images once using the user's existing image-generation subscription, review the deity depiction and export optimized static assets. Generic calendar, Sun/Moon and devotional symbols can use the app's existing icon/vector system. Store a short provenance record beside every new imported/generated asset. Generated artwork should support the app's current cream, saffron and devotional art direction; it does not determine calendar dates.

## Panchak and Gand Mool period coverage

Daily `panchakaPeriods` are the complement of panchang-ts 5.4.0's `inauspicious.panchakaRahita` intervals within the local Hindu day, from sunrise to the following sunrise. The library uses a normalized sidereal Moon longitude of **300° through the 360° wrap**: Dhanishtha's third pada through Revati. A `panchaka` boolean is only a sunrise snapshot; period arrays also capture spells beginning later in the day. The source's daily period function supplies the boundary instants; the app does not fit or invent a Panchak endpoint. See the [daily Panchang API](https://dharmagya.app/docs/panchang/daily-panchang) and the installed `src/core/panchaka.ts` / `src/core/panchakaRahita.ts` implementation represented in the package bundle.

Daily `gandaMulaPeriods` select nakshatra spans through the library's public `computeGandaMula` classification: Ashwini (0), Ashlesha (8), Magha (9), Jyeshtha (17), Mula (18), and Revati (26). This is **Gand Mool**, not just Mula nakshatra. Their boundaries come from the same calculated nakshatra transitions used in daily details. Returned `start` and `end` are clipped to the selected sunrise-to-sunrise day, with `clippedStart` / `clippedEnd` flags; Gand Mool records also retain `actualStart` / `actualEnd` when known. Times and names are bilingual. Polar dates without a complete solar cycle return empty arrays.

Month cards may merge adjacent coverage of the same kind, allowing the source's one-millisecond transition rounding. Such cards describe coverage **within the selected Hindu days/month**. Coverage touching a month boundary must be labelled as continuing, rather than presented as the actual start or finish of the whole spell. The sampled Delhi October 2026 Panchak boundaries are approximately 21 October 07:01 to 25 October 19:23 IST; the reference calendar image shows about 07:00 and 19:22. Tests check the 300° crossing, both daily clipping directions, all six Gand Mool nakshatras, adjacent Revati/Ashwini spans, and empty polar results.
